# AEC firm knowledge: domain notes for a firm-memory feature

Research date: October 30, 2026. Status: domain evidence only. This note does not propose a MOKE design.

Question: how do architecture, engineering and construction (AEC) firms record and reuse their own ways of working (typical details, calculation templates, sheet and title block standards, QA/QC checklists, recurring review comments), and what do AI products already do with firm-specific knowledge?

Conventions. Each claim carries a source link and a date. "Accessed 2026-10-30" marks an undated page. **Shipped** means the vendor's own release notes, help documentation or general-availability announcement describes the feature as available. **Preview** means the vendor labels it beta, preview or labs. **Announced** means a dated plan with no availability evidence found. **Marketing claim** means a capability stated on a product page that I could not check against documentation or an independent test. Vendor blogs that argue a product category (InspectMind, Helonic, Structured AI, Nomic, SWAPP) are marked as vendor sources even when the point seems reasonable.

## Summary of decision-relevant findings

1. Drawings already carry structured, standard metadata a firm memory could key on: UDS sheet identifiers (discipline, sheet type, sequence), detail indicators that reference a detail number and a sheet, drawing block titles and scales, sheet keynotes and reference keynotes tied to MasterFormat sections, and revision clouds with deltas. Firms adapt these conventions, and the NCS leaves sequence numbering and some sheet types user-defined (Section 1.1).
2. Firms that keep detail libraries separate a vetted "typical" set from historical project details. They retire superseded details with a reason instead of deleting them, and revisit them on code cycles and after RFIs or field problems (Pirros help; Caltrans Revised Standard Plans; Section 1.3). The most common way reuse goes wrong is the copied detail or note that no longer fits: a different seismic design category, a wrong jurisdiction, a superseded code edition, delegated-design language left in by mistake (STRUCTURE magazine 2021–2022; Section 2.2).
3. Under professional-practice rules, reuse does not transfer responsibility. The NCEES Model Rules require a licensee who adapts a standard design to do the code research and design file again. The licensee who seals takes "full professional responsibility" (Section 2.3). Standard details normally remain the firm's property and can be reused across clients under AIA B101 and EJCDC terms. Client confidentiality limits what may flow between projects, and the AIA's 2024 Code of Ethics and its AI toolkit treat client drawings as confidential data (Sections 2.4–2.5).
4. Among the products checked, only Pirros ships retrieval of a firm's past details by the condition they solve, with firm-approved and reference-only status. Its Quality Management System checks whether a detail in the set matches the firm-approved version. Pirros works in Revit, not on PDF sets. Nomic generates a reusable "Drawing Review Standards" document from past markups and RFI logs. Most other reviewers, including UpCodes, Groundbook, InspectMind, CodeComply, Structured AI and Bluebeam Max, add firm knowledge as uploaded checklists or standards documents rather than learning it from history (Section 3).
5. Correction memory, meaning learning from reviewer dismissals and fixes, appears only in marketing: SWAPP "workflow memory", Pirros "learns the firm's tolerance", Pelles "each fix ... teaches it". I found no independent evaluation of any of these claims (Section 3, Section 6).
6. Bluebeam Max went generally available on May 19, 2026, but Smart Review is still labeled preview. It runs a fixed set of checks, is tuned for US vertical commercial floor plans and supports only imperial units. Procore's agent packages have been generally available since July 23, 2026. The Procore feature that teaches agents company standards, Skills, was announced to roll out from August 2026 (Section 3).
7. Research and benchmarks show models read drawing text reliably but read symbols and follow cross-sheet references poorly. AECV-Bench reports text questions at up to 0.95 accuracy and symbol counting at roughly 0.40–0.55. On LED-BENCH, the best model averages 52.65%. Detail-to-detail similarity retrieval for AEC typical details has no public benchmark that I found (Section 4).
8. Practitioners and surveys stress the same requirements. A finding should name its sheet and location and cite its basis (code section, spec paragraph, the sheets that disagree). It should carry a severity, and the reviewer's accept or reject should be recorded with a reason. False positives cost more than their count suggests because triage time grows faster than their number. AIA (2025) and Autodesk (2025) surveys report near-universal accuracy concern and falling trust in AI (Section 5).

## 1. How firm standards are encoded

### 1.1 US National CAD Standard (NCS) and Uniform Drawing System (UDS)

- **NCS version.** NCS V7 "released in 2025" adds a floor-numbering format, survey and mapping discipline designators and revised notation text ([NCS V7 what's new](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/new.php), accessed 2026-10-30). The ordering page lists the V7 online document under NIBS product code 92014-7 with a 2024 ISBN date ([NCS V7 ordering](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/ordering.php), accessed 2026-10-30). The UDS is CSI's module within the NCS ([CSI NCS page](https://www.csiresources.org/standards/ncs), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **UDS modules.** The UDS modules cover drawing set organization (set order, sheet identification, file naming), sheet organization (drawing area, title block, coordinate grid), schedules, drafting conventions (including cross-referencing), terms and abbreviations, symbols (indexed by MasterFormat 2004 number plus a three-digit extension) and notations ([NCS V7 content](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/content.php), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Sheet identifier format.** A sheet ID is a discipline designator (one letter plus a hyphen, or two letters), a one-digit sheet type and a two-digit sequence number. Sheet types: 0 general, 1 plans, 2 elevations, 3 sections, 4 large-scale views, 5 details, 6 schedules and diagrams, 7 and 8 user-defined (type 7 explicitly "including typical detail sheets"), 9 3D. Sequence numbers start at 01, need not be consecutive, and inserted sheets may take a suffix of up to three characters ([NCS V5 UDS Module 1](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs5/pdfs/ncs5_uds1.pdf); [NCS V6 UDS Module 1](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs6/pdfs/ncs6_uds1.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). The NCS FAQ notes that sequence numbers are user-defined. Matching floor numbers to sheet numbers (A-102 as the second floor) is an option, not a rule ([NCS V7 FAQ](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/faqs.php), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Layer names.** Layer names follow discipline, then a mandatory four-character major group, up to two optional minor groups and an optional status field, for example `A-WALL-FULL-TEXT`. Annotation goes under `ANNO` (`DIMS`, `TEXT`, `TTLB` for title block, `REVS` for revisions) ([NCS V5 layer format](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs5/pdfs/ncs5_clg_lnf.pdf); [Spangler, NIBS 2017](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/pdfs/spangler_02_09_2017.pdf)). Layers exist only in CAD files. A PDF packet usually preserves them as optional content groups or loses them. That is my inference, not a sourced fact.
- **Symbols and notes.** The symbols module includes detail indicators (dashed circle, dashed rectangle, and a 45° arrow for small conditions), the drawing block title and the door mark ([NCS V6 UDS Module 6](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs6/pdfs/ncs6_uds6.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). Sheet keynotes must use a hexagon. A reference keynote is "the root of the specification section number and a user-defined suffix". A revision is shown by "a cloud and a delta symbol" ([NCS V7 FAQ](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs7/faqs.php)). The notations module says notes "have important legal consequences" and should avoid vague references such as "SEE SPECIFICATIONS" ([NCS V6 UDS Module 7](https://www.nationalcadstandard.org/ncs6/pdfs/ncs6_uds7.pdf)).
- **Agency adaptations.** Agencies amend the NCS. The VA Drawing Deliverable Requirements limit scales to the NCS common-scales list, require a graphic scale for each scaled view and make keynoting optional ([VA DDR](https://www.cfm.va.gov/til/bim/DwgDelivRqmts.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). The Los Alamos CAD Standards Manual numbers details and letters sections independently on each sheet, reading from upper left to lower right. References carry the sheet number, and deleted numbers may be left blank. Standard details carry their own `ST-` numbers assigned by the Engineering Standards Manager ([LANL CSM 200 R5](https://engstandards.lanl.gov/CSM/pdfs/CSM200-R5.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). This is the convention behind a reference like "5/S-501": detail 5 on sheet S-501.

### 1.2 Firm CAD and BIM standards files and markup libraries

- **AutoCAD standards checks.** AutoCAD audits drawings against a `.dws` standards file covering layers, dimension styles, linetypes and text styles. CHECKSTANDARDS checks one drawing and the Batch Standards Checker writes an HTML report. Users can fix or ignore each violation ([AutoCAD 2026 help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2026/ENU/AutoCAD-Core/files/GUID-F8B47FBB-1276-4D74-BA89-0440CB4E4866.htm); [Autodesk blog, 2026-01-06](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/autocad/cad-standards-tuesday-tips-with-frank/)).
- **Autodesk Assistant standards check.** Autodesk Assistant in AutoCAD 2027 adds "ability to check standards of the current drawing against a standards file" with the caveat "Mistakes are possible" (**shipped**; [AutoCAD 2027 what's new](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/AutoCAD-WhatsNew/files/GUID-99E2D8F7-D4D9-4B3A-9E57-7B73D6BB12AD.htm), accessed 2026-10-30). In Civil 3D 2027 the same check runs as a technology preview. It compares the drawing to a company `.dwt` and produces a report, and the user must make the changes manually ([IMAGINiT, 2026-08-05](https://resources.imaginit.com/civil-solutions-blog/autodesk-assistant-standards-checker)).
- **Bluebeam Tool Chest.** Bluebeam Revu stores standard markups in Tool Chest tool sets (`.btx`). A shared tool set on a network drive is locked by default, checked out for edits and cached for offline use. Bluebeam describes sharing "for setting standard markups for different projects, job functions, clients, and more" ([Bluebeam Manage tool sets](https://support.bluebeam.com/user-manual/menus/window/manage-tool-sets.html); [Share tool sets](https://support.bluebeam.com/revu/how-to/share-tool-sets.html), accessed 2026-10-30). Firms use these libraries to standardize review comments. Markup lists can be exported, and Smart Review exports map to the Markups List columns (Section 3).
- **Revit tagging templates.** BIMLOGIQ Smart Annotation saves firm tagging setups as exportable templates and can generate one from an existing view ([BIMLOGIQ](https://bimlogiq.com/product/smart-annotation.html), accessed 2026-10-30). SWAPP describes typical firm guideline documents. They cover tag placement, door-mark numbering derived from room numbers, completeness of tagging, dimension-string rules and sheet and view conventions, and SWAPP says they usually "sit in a PDF nobody opens" ([SWAPP, 2026-10-16](https://swapp.ai/enforce-revit-drawing-standards-firm-guidelines/), vendor source).

### 1.3 Standard and typical detail libraries

- **Organization.** Detail libraries are commonly organized by CSI MasterFormat. Land F/X ships MasterFormat-based division lists that update every 30 days while keeping office edits separate ([Land F/X docs](https://www.landfx.com/docs/details/organize/5962-standard-library-division-list.html), accessed 2026-10-30). CSI's MasterFormat guide allows lower-level numbers for "multiple versions of the same specification section for different clients or for different types of projects" in a firm library, presented on projects under the standard number ([Using MasterFormat](https://theconstructionstandard.com/guides/masterformat-using.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Vetted versus historical details.** Pirros help pages describe a vetted "Typical Details" set separate from "All Details" drawn from uploaded projects. A "Reference Only" status lets staff see past conditions "without the risk of reusing Details that haven't been reviewed". Search covers view names, sheet names, titles on sheets and annotation text. Project filters cover location, market sector, client and building material ([Pirros: search for details](https://help.pirros.com/en/articles/14503491-how-to-search-for-details), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Vetting and maintenance.** Pirros recommends updating a typical when "a code cycle changes (e.g., new IBC edition)", when "an RFI or field issue reveals a detailing error", when the firm adopts a new material or method, or when a team member flags a problem. Retired details stay in the library as historical references with a note such as "Superseded by updated version per 2024 IBC". Suggested cadence: review flags monthly, audit quarterly, and review against current codes annually. Pirros notifies anyone who downloaded the old version ([Pirros: managing your library](https://help.pirros.com/en/articles/14502009-managing-your-detail-library-over-time), accessed 2026-10-30). Its bootstrap guide treats a detail that appears five or more times across a firm's 10–25 best projects as a strong typical candidate, "effectively been reviewed eight times". It routes a redline review to the person who handles construction administration, because "they know which detail draws an RFI every time" ([Pirros, 2026-06-27](https://help.pirros.com/en/articles/15676361-build-a-typical-detail-library-from-scratch-stash-vet-and-refine-with-mira)).
- **Public-agency analog.** Caltrans publishes Standard Plans by edition plus "Revised Standard Plans" (RSP), each with a plan number and date. Canceled plans are listed explicitly ("Canceled Plan P8, 4/20/2026"), and each RSP sheet states that it "supplements the Standard Plans book dated 2024" ([Caltrans Revised 2025 Standard Plans](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/design/xxxx-ccs-standard-plans-and-standard-specifications/revised-2025-standard-plans-toc), last updated 2026-04-20; [2024 RSP PDF](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/design/documents/entire-2024-revised-standard-plans-5-sheets-locked-a11y.pdf)).

### 1.4 Master specifications

- **MasterSpec and office masters.** MasterSpec is owned by the AIA and published exclusively by Deltek. Specpoint lists more than 900 specifications ([Deltek help](https://help.deltek.com/Products/Specpoint/AE/MasterSpec.html), accessed 2026-10-30). Deltek defines an office master as a custom section that "enables a firm to preserve its knowledge". Firms may keep several office-master versions of one section "to cater to projects of different project groups, owners, and submission and regulatory requirements" ([Deltek Office Masters](https://help.deltek.com/products/Specpoint/AE/OfficeMasters.html), accessed 2026-10-30). An AIA and Deltek article warns that Word-based office masters lead teams to work "from the latest authored section from the last project rather than standardized vetted information" ([AIA, 2022-09-13](https://www.aia.org/resource-center/proven-specification-strategies-better-built-projects)).
- **UFGS and SpecsIntact.** The Unified Facilities Guide Specifications (USACE, NAVFAC, AFCEC) are published for SpecsIntact and updated quarterly. The 2026 releases were February 19, May 28, August 27 and November 19 ([WBDG UFGS Masters](https://www.wbdg.org/dod/specsintact/masters), accessed 2026-10-30). "Archived" sections are "prohibited for use in new projects" ([WBDG UFGS library](https://www.wbdg.org/dod/ufgs), updated 2026-08-25). NASA withdrew from UFGS on September 30, 2023 ([WBDG NASA](https://www.wbdg.org/nasa/master-specifications), posted 2023-11-30). Agency masters thus carry explicit effective dates and active or archived status, which firm masters often lack.

### 1.5 Calculation templates and design guides

- **IStructE spreadsheet guidance.** The Institution of Structural Engineers says "a well-checked and validated spreadsheet, reused across multiple projects, can enhance structural safety. However, if a spreadsheet contains errors, reusing it can significantly increase risk." It asks for cited clause references, separated inputs, calculations and outputs, edge-case tests, and a recorded creator, last modifier and last checker ([IStructE](https://www.istructe.org/resources/technology-digital/calculation-models-spreadsheets/), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **CalcTree.** CalcTree separates per-project design inputs, company templates (versioned, "change rarely") and project calculations that are reviewed and signed. It argues for engineer-controlled rather than automatic propagation of input changes ([CalcTree, 2026-06-21](https://www.calctree.com/blog/project-level-calculation-management); [Templates 2.0, 2026-10-08](https://www.calctree.com/blog/templates2-0), vendor source).

### 1.6 QA/QC checklists and review records

- **CASE 962-D.** The Council of American Structural Engineers (CASE) publishes _A Guideline Addressing Coordination and Completeness of Structural Construction Documents_ (CASE 962-D, 2020 edition) plus drawing review checklists ([ACEC CASE publications](https://www.acec.org/member-center/get-involved/coalitions/case/resources/case-publications/), accessed 2026-10-30). The earlier edition's appendix checklist of more than 350 items was meant "as a starting point in establishing your own in-house checklist", used "as a 'to do' list rather than a 'did I complete' list" ([STRUCTURE, February 2004](https://www.concreteproductivity.org/portals/19/Images/D-CASEbusinesspractices1.pdf)).
- **Engineers and Geoscientists BC.** The BC regulator requires documented checks whose record states who checked, when, what was checked, the issues found and the corrections made ([EGBC documented checks guide](https://www.egbc.ca/getmedia/5dea60e7-a232-4905-a064-b0468006e953/EGBC-QMG-Documented-Checks.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). Firm quality practice there includes retaining project documentation for at least 10 years ([ACEC-BC, 2022-11-02](https://acec-bc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2022-11-02_Budget-Guidelines-for-Eng-Services-Doc-2_Buildings-1.pdf)).
- **Owner checklists.** Owner checklists ask whether previous submission comments were addressed. The Pennsylvania Turnpike discipline quality verification checklist includes "Addresses previous submission comments" and "Independent Peer review comments reviewed and addressed" ([PA Turnpike](https://files.paturnpike.com/production/docs/default-source/resources/policies-standards/engineering-standards/dom-resources/discipline-quality-verification-checklist.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Stutzman and Medcalf (2018).** In a 2018 NIBS BEST5 paper, two specification consultants wrote that firm standards "all too often, relate primarily to formatting rather than content". They found that "there is not a plan for periodic reviews of standard details", so details no longer meet current requirements. They recommend Pareto-style checklists informed by QC reviewer and construction administration feedback, early checking of initial details "to avoid the 'ripple' effect of copying mistakes", and libraries of "Lessons Learned" technical bulletins. They also "advocate destruction of documents with review comments and checklists to avoid unnecessary 'second-guessing'" while keeping a permanent record that the review happened ([Stutzman and Medcalf, NIBS BEST5, 2018-04-16](https://www.conspectusinc.com/blog/2018/04/construction-document-quality-crisis); [PDF](https://www.brikbase.org/sites/default/files/MO1A.1Stutzman.pdf)). This recommendation runs directly against retaining a comment history.

## 2. Firm knowledge management in AEC

### 2.1 Lessons-learned research

- **Caldas et al. (2009), 70 organizations.** Lessons-learned programs were multiplying, but "the potential for savings and improvement has not been fully met". The study identified seven characteristics of an effective program ([J. Constr. Eng. Manage. 135(6), 2009](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%29CO.1943-7862.0000011)).
- **Graham and Thomas (2007), Irish contractor case study.** Reported challenges include "failure to deliver lessons when and where they are needed" and "lack of proper classification system". The firm needed notifications of new lessons and training on its lessons database ([WIT repository, 2007](http://repository.wit.ie/526/1/LESSONS_LEARNED_PRACTICES-_CASE_STUDY_OF_A_LEADING_IRISH_CONSTRUCTION_ORGANIZATION.pdf)).
- **Paranagamage et al. (2012), UK contractors.** There is "a disjoint in the effort spent obtaining lessons learned and their dissemination and use". Respondents named time pressure, reluctance to share problems, and exercises "too generic to be of value" ([Built Environment Project and Asset Management, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/21573727.2012.681643)).
- **Wiewiora and Murphy (2013).** Lessons are often "documented as simple, line-item statements devoid of context". The authors report poor visibility and "overloaded LL spreadsheets" ([Knowledge Management Research & Practice, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1057/kmrp.2013.26)).
- **Australian contractor survey (2013).** The top barriers were employee time, resources and clear guidelines. Only a minority of firms retained lessons-learned documentation for each project ([Construction Innovation 14(1)](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/mcb/ci/2013/00000014/00000001/art00007)).
- **Debs and Hubbard (2023), US construction companies.** Post-project evaluations are frequent, but the information "is not effectively reutilized". Their review cites Carrillo, Ruikar and Fuller (2013), in which lessons-learned documents ranked sixth of seven sharing tools, and lists legal issues and lack of trust as barriers ([AJCEB 23(1/2), 2023-07-12](https://doi.org/10.5130/ajceb.v23i1/2.8390)).

### 2.2 Why reuse fails

- **Practitioner account (STRUCTURE, December 2021 and January 2022).** A delegated-connection engineer lists these failure modes ([Part 1](https://www.structuremag.org/article/the-hidden-cost-of-copy-and-paste-2/); [Part 2, 2022-01-01](https://www.structuremag.org/article/the-hidden-cost-of-copy-and-paste/)):
  - connection details copied from a Seismic Design Category E building to an SDC A project
  - copied details that reference delegated design when none is delegated
  - a steel stair specification on an Arkansas project requiring a California-licensed engineer
  - general notes citing the 1979 welding code, the 1997 building code and the 7th and 8th edition AISC manuals
  - an EOR who demanded joist calculations to the 1989 9th edition while the state had adopted AISC 360-10
- **Spec and checklist consultants (2018).** Standard details go unreviewed between projects, and copy errors "ripple" through a set ([Stutzman and Medcalf, 2018](https://www.conspectusinc.com/blog/2018/04/construction-document-quality-crisis)).
- **Propagation mechanism (vendor article).** A pasted detail "transfers the assumption of review without performing any review". Its callouts may point to sheets that no longer exist and its spec sections may be missing from the project manual. Revisions to the source detail do not reach the copies ([InspectMind, 2026-08-26](https://www.inspectmind.ai/resources/articles/how-copy-paste-detail-errors-spread-across-a-drawing-set)).
- **Agency practice.** Agency libraries mark archived sections as prohibited for new projects and attach dates to every revision (Sections 1.3 and 1.4). Firm libraries often lack the equivalent fields: code edition, jurisdiction, date and status. That conclusion is my synthesis, not a single source.

### 2.3 Engineer of record and responsible charge

- **Sealing.** NCEES Model Rules §240.20 (August 2024) require the seal "only when [work] was under the licensee's responsible charge". Responsible charge means authority to change, reject or approve the work, personal awareness of the project's scope and limitations, the ability to answer questions about engineering decisions, and full responsibility. Revisions to sealed documents must be "described and dated" ([NCEES Model Rules, August 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Model-Rules_August-2024_web.pdf)).
- **Standard designs.** When "the work is a site adaptation of a standard design plan", a successor licensee "may take responsible charge by performing all professional services to include developing a complete design file with work or design criteria, calculations, code research, and any necessary and appropriate changes" (same source, §240.20.G).
- **State example.** Texas lets engineers seal "standards or general guideline specifications that they have reviewed and selected. Upon sealing, engineers take full professional responsibility for that work" ([22 Tex. Admin. Code §137.33](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-137-33), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Ethics opinion.** The NSPE Board of Ethical Review found it unethical to seal manufacturer drawings not prepared under the engineer's direction and control ([NSPE BER Case 19-11, approved 2020-01-30](https://www.nspe.org/sites/default/files/BER19-11-APPROVED.pdf)).

### 2.4 Ownership and liability of reused documents

- **AIA B101-2017.** The architect and consultants retain copyright in instruments of service. The owner receives "a nonexclusive license to use ... solely and exclusively for purposes of constructing, using, maintaining, altering and adding to the Project", and unauthorized use is "at the Owner's sole risk" (§7.2–7.4; [B101-2017 sample](https://bidcondocs.delaware.gov/DTCC/DTCC_18001Asblghvac_sample_1.pdf); [AIA Contract Docs help](https://help.aiacontracts.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009310881-the-rights-of-an-architect-s-instruments-of-service), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **Keeping standard details.** The AIA advises excluding "all drawings, designs, details, specifications, and all other materials you prepared prior to commencing services" from any copyright transfer, so the firm can keep reusing them ([AIA, 2023-12-01](https://www.aia.org/resource-center/understanding-copyright-protection-architects)). A model clause preserves the consultant's "practice detail library" for reuse "for other clients" ([ConstructionRisk contract guide](https://www.constructionrisk.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/Contracts-for-Design-Professionals-by-C-Risk-Module-6.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30). EJCDC language keeps "the right to reuse at the discretion of the Engineer" ([IRMI, 2002](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/copyright-infringement-of-design-documents)).
- **Insurer guidance.** The insurer Victor O. Schinnerer recommends that "the instruments of service shall not be reused ... for any new project without an evaluation of the documents in relation to applicable codes and standards" ([Schinnerer management advisory, via Professional Underwriters, 2024](https://www.profunderwriters.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Electronic-Transfer-Issues.pdf)).

### 2.5 Client confidentiality

- **AIA Code of Ethics (2024).** Rule 3.401 bars knowingly disclosing information "that would adversely affect their client or that they have been asked to maintain in confidence" ([AIA Code of Ethics, April 2024](https://www.aia.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/aia-code-of-ethics-april-2024.pdf)).
- **AIA AI Firm Toolkit.** The toolkit classifies "Firm standards, internal templates" as Internal data, limited to approved enterprise AI tools. "Project drawings not yet public" are Confidential, allowed only in tools under a verified data-handling agreement. Security-critical drawings (data centers, courthouses, embassies, utilities) go into no AI tool "without explicit legal and client sign-off". Restricted uses include "relying on AI-generated code references ... without independent verification" and "using AI in ways prohibited by an active client contract" ([AIA AI Firm Toolkit](https://aifirmtoolkit.aia.org/), accessed 2026-10-30).
- **ACEC guidelines (July 2024).** ACEC recommends human QA/QC by the person in responsible charge, and keeping AI outputs from growing "so large or complex that employees cannot understand and review them thoroughly" ([ACEC AI guidelines](https://www.acec.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Artificial-Intelligence-Guidelines-for-ACEC-AI-Risk-Subcommittee-Technology-Committee-July-2024.pdf)). A 2024 SGH and Donovan Hatem memo advises avoiding inputs "that could potentially enable an outsider to identify the specific project" ([ACEC-MA, 2024-04-16](https://www.acecma.org/wp-content/uploads/SGH-And-Donovan-Hatem-Guidelines-For-AI-Use.pdf)).
- **Contract terms.** Contracts can impose project-specific confidentiality. Document Crunch's contract playbook asks "Are there confidentiality requirements on this project?" ([Document Crunch, 2025-01-29](https://www.documentcrunch.com/blog/project-playbooks)).
- **Gap.** I found no source that addresses whether showing one client's project detail to a team working for another client is permitted. See Section 6.

### 2.6 Vendor data terms

- **Autodesk.** Autodesk's December 2025 Acceptable Use Policy update says customers "may train on your models and data", provided they do not copy Autodesk products ([Autodesk, 2025-12-08](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/news/autodesk-terms-changes/); [AEC Magazine analysis, 2026-03-03](https://aecmag.com/features/rewriting-the-rules/)). Autodesk says some features train on "elements of customer content in aggregated and de-identified form" and that it does not share customer data for third-party model training ([Autodesk, 2026-08-17](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/stories/how-autodesk-builds-trusted-ai/)).
- **Specialist vendors.** These say they do not train on customer data: Pirros ("Zero AI training on your data"), SWAPP ("not shared across firms"), UpCodes, Structured AI and Pelles (product pages cited in Section 3).

Patterns across products (my synthesis of the table):

- Firm knowledge enters these products in four ways:
  1. uploaded standards documents or checklists (most reviewers)
  2. curated libraries with approval status (Pirros, Deltek office masters)
  3. standards synthesized from past markups and RFIs (Nomic; Procore's Lessons Learned Agent)
  4. correction memory (SWAPP, Pirros, Pelles; marketing only)
- I found no shipped PDF reviewer that suggests "your firm used detail X on a similar project" during review. Pirros comes closest, but it works inside Revit.
- Nearly every product cites a sheet location and keeps a human accept-or-reject step. Several say they do not train on customer data.

## 4. Research on drawing and detail retrieval

**Title block and sheet parsing**

- **Lombardi et al. (2025, Buro Happold and Birmingham City University).** A lightweight CNN plus GPT-4o detects title blocks and extracts metadata from CAD and hand-drawn drawings. A search interface built on the results was deployed on real projects, and the work includes an expert-annotated dataset ([arXiv 2504.08645, 2025-04-11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08645); [EC3 2025](https://doi.org/10.35490/ec3.2025.344)).
- **Peng et al. (EC3 2026).** Qwen2.5-VL-3B fine-tuned with LoRA locates title blocks by grounded prompting, then extracts scale, drawing name and route from historical bridge drawings. Dense machine-printed title blocks are harder than handwritten ones ([EC3 2026 paper](https://ec-3.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EC32026_252.pdf)).
- **Earlier work.** Kashevnik et al. reached about 74% accuracy on title block detection and metadata for good-quality scans ([IEEE Access, 2023-02-14](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2023.3244603)). Sajadfar et al. applied Faster R-CNN to title blocks in construction documents ([SSRN, 2024](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4845684)).

**Drawing similarity and retrieval**

- **Dai et al. (WWW 2019).** In engineering diagram search, a query part matches "only a small region". Local matching beats global matching, and a gating network suppresses "visual metadata such as callouts" ([ACM, 2019-05-13](https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313500)).
- **DeepPatent (WACV 2022).** The dataset has 350,000+ design-patent drawings. The authors found commercial image retrieval performs poorly and "retrieval of technical drawings remains an open challenge" ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/papers/Kucer_DeepPatent_Large_Scale_Patent_Drawing_Recognition_and_Retrieval_WACV_2022_paper.pdf)). A sketch-based transfer-learning approach for diagram retrieval appears in [Bhattarai et al., CVPRW 2020](https://www.openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w8/Bhattarai_Diagram_Image_Retrieval_Using_Sketch-Based_Deep_Learning_and_Transfer_Learning_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf).
- **Manufacturing precedents.** Several systems retrieve past drawings with partial-design queries or multimodal inputs: [Van Daele et al., AAAI 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17783) (deployed tool, query by part of a drawing, table or text); [CoLIBRi](https://dbs.uni-leipzig.de/research/publications/colibri-supporting-quotation-through-multi-modal-retrieval-and-conversational) (OCR, segmentation, visual embeddings and a VLM for title blocks; code and weights released; accessed 2026-10-30); a CLIP vector search ([IET, 2026-06-01](https://doi.org/10.1049/icp.2026.1931)); and [Baba et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1109/ism.2005.105).
- **BIM detail reuse.** An association-rule recommender built from more than 30,000 BIM views of a case-study firm reported 80% efficacy and 15% workflow time saved ([2021 case study](https://www.academia.edu/65122864/Artificial_intelligence_for_BIM_content_management_and_delivery_Case_study_of_association_rule_mining_for_construction_detailing)). BIM search with geometric, semantic and topological embeddings is described in [Huang et al., Buildings, 2025-03-18](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15060951). A case-based reasoning system over BCF coordination issues reached over 60% top-1 retrieval of similar cases and 70% acceptance of the resolutions it proposed ([TUM thesis, 2025](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/1788972)).

**Symbols and legends**

- **Few-shot detection.** Few-shot symbol detection works with fewer than ten training examples per class ([Jamieson et al., 2024-09-29](https://doi.org/10.1080/08839514.2024.2406712)).
- **Legend-informed recognition.** Contrastive learning on legend entries reaches over 80% accuracy on building-services diagrams and rejects symbols that are not in the legend ([Hain et al., 2025-10-03](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-72243-5_23)). Legend-table extraction followed by symbol counting is described in [Sarkar, arXiv 2204.13277](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.13277).
- **BlueprintSymVL.** This benchmark gives the model a one-shot visual exemplar because "standardized symbology ... rarely hold[s]" in practice ([Chalmers, accessed 2026-10-30](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/549362/file/549362_Fulltext.pdf)).

**Datasets and benchmarks**

- **FloorPlanCAD.** 15,663 SVG floor plans (November 2021 update). The project shut down in 2022 ([site](https://floorplancad.github.io/)).
- **ArchCAD-400K (NeurIPS 2025).** 5,538 drawings, 413,062 chunks and 27 categories including notation, with annotations derived from CAD layer and block structure. A 40K-sample subset was released first ([NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/b96ce7d38339874a8704e8895f743284-Paper-Conference.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/ArchiAI-LAB/ArchCAD)).
- **AEC-Bench (Nomic, 2026-03-31).** 196 tasks on public project PDFs across three scopes: within a sheet, across a drawing set, and across project documents. Tasks include detail title accuracy, note callout accuracy, cross-reference resolution and tracing, sheet index consistency and spec-drawing sync, with defects injected by domain experts. General coding agents "fail on visual grounding, exhaustive traversal, and cross-document coordination". Released under Apache 2 ([arXiv 2603.29199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29199); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nomic-ai/aec-bench)).
- **AECV-Bench (2026-01-08).** OCR and text questions reach up to 0.95 accuracy. Counting doors and windows "remains unsolved (often 0.40–0.55)" ([arXiv 2601.04819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819)).
- **DrawingVQA (CVPR 2026 Findings).** 33 issued-for-construction structural drawings and 92 questions, with "a substantial gap between model and expert performance" on expert reasoning ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026F/papers/Jung_DrawingVQA_A_Real-World_Benchmark_for_Multi-Depth_Visual-Textual_Reasoning_on_Construction_CVPRF_2026_paper.pdf)).
- **LED-BENCH (2026-10-17).** 107 large-format sheets. The best model averages 52.65%, and all models do worse when evidence is spread across a sheet ([arXiv 2610.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19571)).
- **ArchPlanVQA.** General VLMs reach 33–38% on architectural floor-plan CAD questions ([J. Comput. Civ. Eng., 2026-06-02](https://doi.org/10.1061/jccee5.cpeng-7571)).

**Mining review comments and RFIs**

- **Panahi et al. (2024).** NLP, ChatGPT and computer vision identify prior-project RFIs likely to recur on a project under design review, tested on 19 healthcare projects ([ASCE i3CE, 2024-01-25](https://doi.org/10.1061/9780784485224.020)).
- **Fokwa Soh et al. (2020).** Across 26 steel projects, the recurring RFI theme was missing or poorly presented connection information ([OTMCJ, 2020](https://doi.org/10.2478/otmcj-2020-0005)).
- **Elshaboury et al. (EC3 2025).** LLM extraction from design inspection reports structures each finding as checked document, referenced document, defect and building object. The authors note such reports are usually "inaccessible due to confidentiality constraints" ([EC3 2025](https://doi.org/10.35490/ec3.2025.337)).
- **Afzal et al. (2023).** LDA topic modeling of RFIs ([IEEE CASE, 2023-08-26](https://doi.org/10.1109/case56687.2023.10260517)).

## 5. What reviewers want from AI findings and suggestions

**Surveys**

- **AIA (2025-03-11).** Only 6% of architects use AI regularly. Ninety percent are concerned about inaccuracy, unintended consequences, security, authenticity and transparency ([AIA press release](https://www.aia.org/about-aia/press/new-research-explores-perceptions-and-opportunities-artificial-intelligence)). One respondent wrote that AI output "can look good but be very wrong" ([AIA Architect, 2025-03-03](https://www.aia.org/aia-architect/article/architects-are-excited-about-potential-ai-concerns-abound)).
- **Autodesk State of Design & Make 2025.** Trust in AI among AECO leaders fell 14 points year over year ([Autodesk report PDF, April 2025](https://adsknews.autodesk.com/app/uploads/2025/04/2025-SDM-Report-%E2%80%93-AECO-Final.pdf)).
- **Architects' Journal (2025-10-03).** Inaccuracy was the top barrier, cited by 55%, and "many saying they had to thoroughly check any materials produced" ([AJ](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/ai-in-practice-chatgpt-use-far-outstrips-midjourney-aj-survey-shows)).
- **RIBA AI Report 2025.** Half of respondents disagree that AI has improved specification accuracy. The report warns it is "unlikely to be a legitimate defence to claim the genAI tool is at fault" ([RIBA](https://www.riba.org/media/uqmlay5m/riba-ai-report-2025-final.pdf)).
- **BST Global with ACEC.** 84% of AEC executives are concerned about accuracy, including "incomplete or outdated data" ([BST report](https://bstglobal.com/resources/download/AI_and_Data_Insights_Report/20260226/BST_Global-AI_and_Data_Insights_Report_2025.pdf), survey Oct–Dec 2024).
- **RICS (2025-09-12).** 45% of 2,200+ construction professionals report no AI implementation ([RICS](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-in-construction-report)).

**Evaluation guidance and practitioner writing**

- **redline.best checklist.** A useful finding names a sheet and location, cites its basis, ranks severity, can be accepted or rejected with a reason, and records "which person accepted or rejected each finding". Evaluators should time triage, run the same set twice to measure variability, and score each discipline separately ([redline.best, 2026-09-25](https://redline.best/guides/evaluate-ai-drawing-review-tools)).
- **False positives.** Their cost "is nonlinear": past a point, sorting noise "eats up more time than the tool saved", and a finding that is traceable can be dismissed in seconds ([Structured AI](https://getstructured.ai/education/false-positives-in-ai-drawing-review/), vendor source, accessed 2026-10-30). RedlineBench scores a false alarm at −1 against +1 for a correct find, on a project with 55 known issues ([RedlineBench, 2026-03-16](https://redlinebench.benfeicht.com/)).
- **Industrial P&ID deployment.** Dzhusupova et al. treat false positives as tolerable because engineers review every output, and false negatives as missed design errors ([J. Softw. Evol. Process, 2023-02-15](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/smr.2543)).
- **Accountability.** A framework separates reading, comprehension, cross-sheet understanding and application. It argues that a judgment at the application level needs an attributable sign-off before it drives action ([KP Reddy, 2026-07-16](https://kpreddy.substack.com/p/a-framework-for-evaluating-ai-in)).
- **What QC reviewers value.** Identifying "what is not shown, but is required" is often the most valuable thing a reviewer provides. Checklists should be short and fed by QC and construction administration feedback ([Stutzman and Medcalf, 2018](https://www.conspectusinc.com/blog/2018/04/construction-document-quality-crisis)).
- **Professional duty.** The AIA restricts relying on AI code references without verification, and ACEC warns against output too large to review (Section 2.5).

Requirements these sources imply for suggestions drawn from prior work (my inference from the evidence above, not a design):

- Each suggestion should identify its source: project, sheet, detail, revision, approval status, code edition and jurisdiction.
- A suggestion should say why it applies here.
- Nothing should reach a reviewer that the reviewer cannot check quickly.
- The person who accepts or rejects a suggestion should be recorded.

## 6. Open uncertainties

1. **Learning from corrections is unverified.** SWAPP, Pirros (learned tolerance) and Pelles describe it, and I found no independent test, documentation of the mechanism, or failure data. Nomic's standards-from-markups workflow is documented, but I found no measure of its quality.
2. **Cross-client reuse inside AI tools.** The AIA code, the AIA toolkit and ACEC guidance address disclosure to AI vendors. None addresses showing client A's past project detail to a team working for client B, or what "similar project" retrieval does under per-project confidentiality clauses. B101 lets firms reuse their own standard details, but project-specific content is licensed to the owner for that project only.
3. **Retaining review comments.** Stutzman and Medcalf (2018) advocate destroying commented review sets once the review is recorded. EGBC-style rules require documented checks and roughly 10-year retention. I found no legal source on whether an AI-searchable history of internal review comments increases discovery exposure.
4. **NCS adoption and version.** I found no data on what share of US firms follow the NCS and UDS sheet format faithfully. The V7 "released in 2025" statement and the 2024 ISBN date conflict slightly. Firm deviations such as three-digit sequences (LANL) and floor-number alignment are documented anecdotally only.
5. **Vendor status may have moved.** Bluebeam Smart Review is still labeled preview, and its accuracy outside US commercial floor plans is unknown. I did not verify whether Procore Skills reached customers after August 2026, or whether Graphisoft's AI Assistant was released in October 2025.
6. **No benchmark for detail similarity in AEC.** No public dataset labels typical details by the condition they solve (for example "1-hr metal stud partition head at deck") across firms. Pirros's condition search is proprietary. Existing benchmarks measure reading and cross-reference tasks, not whether a retrieved past detail applies to a new project.
7. **No evidence on what applicability metadata firms actually record.** Code edition, seismic design category, jurisdiction and fire rating are what a reviewer needs to judge whether a past detail still applies. No source showed how often firm libraries record these fields; Pirros's project filters (location, market sector, client, material) are the only documented example.
8. **Most 2026 product evidence is vendor-authored.** Accuracy figures from Structured AI, UpCodes, Provision, Helonic and Trunk Tools are self-reported. Independent comparisons such as RedlineBench and AEC-Bench are small, with 196 or fewer items.
