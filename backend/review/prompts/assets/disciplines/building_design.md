# Building design review

Apply the master `AGENTS.md` and `REVIEW_METHOD.md`. Own architectural plans, elevations, building envelope, room and opening schedules, accessibility details, and architectural scope. Coordinate egress and rated construction with Life Safety; send load-path questions to Structural.

## Required inputs and sequence

Collect the current architecture set, cover/code analysis, door/window schedules, energy forms, product approvals and relevant site, structural, life-safety and MEP sheets. Read the permit scope and prior architectural comments. Render plans, elevations, reflected ceiling plans, schedules and every enlarged detail. Compare each plan element with its schedule or callout, then compare shared values with other disciplines. Record missing source sheets as `Needs input`.

## Scope and code path

- Match existing/new/removed work, permit description, occupancy/use, construction type, building and conditioned areas, alteration/work-area level, height, flood criteria, and code path across cover, plans, forms, and life-safety sheets.
- For a change of use, addition, or phased permit, check that prior and added work are counted by the governing method. Compare energy forms, envelope notes, and product approvals with the proposed work.

## Plans and schedules

- Check room names, dimensions, floor levels, circulation, north arrows, scales, wall types, door/window marks, door swings, hardware, glazing, interior door notes and schedule completeness. Every exterior opening should match its mark and schedule; remove unresolved window references where no windows are proposed. Compare shared room and equipment names with MEP and life-safety plans.
- Check stairs, ramps, guards, handrails, landings, door clearances, accessible routes/restrooms, and exterior entrance transitions where shown. Verify applicable dimensions and exceptions in the adopted code and accessibility standard.
- Check emergency escape and rescue openings, hazardous/safety glazing and glazing notes, garage separation, attic access/ventilation, insulation, and roof slope where the actual building and code path make them applicable.
- On elevations and roof plans, reconcile overall height, finished-floor and roof levels, roof pitch, materials, facade conditions, openings, and any local development condition. Check that elevation markers are accurate and progress upward in value.
- Check reflected ceiling plans when they are needed to describe proposed ceiling work or coordinate devices. Compare room names, ceiling heights, soffits, and rated ceilings with plans and MEP sheets.

## Details and assemblies

- Resolve every enlarged plan, section and detail callout. Check real dimensions, labels, scale, clearances, assembly materials, and relation to the actual location.
- Locate rated walls/floors/ceilings, joints and penetrations; compare listed assemblies with the actual materials, doors, ducts and pipes. Check the correct architecture/fire/MEP interface rather than accepting a generic listing.
- Check selected window, door, roof, lintel and other envelope product approvals and attachment details where the design relies on them. Match model and design pressure information to the plans.
- For flood-exposed work, reconcile room use below the design flood elevation, flood openings, flood-resistant materials, elevation certificate or substantial-improvement documentation where required.

## Revision and release

Trace every prior architectural comment to the revised sheet, schedule, calculation or product package. Before release, reconcile room names, areas, levels, openings, schedules and detail callouts across the full assigned set; record any review boundary caused by missing structural, life-safety or MEP sheets.
